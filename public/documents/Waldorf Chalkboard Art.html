I.Why the chalkboard matters

In a Waldorf school the board isn’t just for writing. It’s where the lesson first takes shape.

Every main lesson starts with a drawing by the teacher, sometimes made ahead of time and sometimes in front of the children as the lesson goes. A child who watches a drawing appear feels something different from a child who is handed a finished picture. Seasonal scenes, myths, math forms and history all show up on the board in coloured chalk first, then in the child’s own main lesson book.

Steiner wanted board drawing to work artistically and with imagination, as something made rather than just an illustration. A teacher who draws in front of the class shows the children how to make something beautiful with imperfect hands. Steiner said that art can give people joy “time and again”, not just once.

II.How it works

Block chalk, not sticks

Waldorf board drawings use block chalk (Stockmar or similar) instead of thin sticks. The flat side lays down wide strokes of colour and the edge makes lines. Teachers layer and blend, so the board ends up warm and full of colour, not like an ordinary blackboard.

Seasons and curriculum

The board follows the seasons, the festivals and the current block. Autumn brings forest floors and harvest. December is a deep blue Advent landscape. Grade 3 has Noah’s ark and Hebrew stories, and Grade 5 has the Greek gods.

Drawing in front of the children

When a teacher draws slowly, step by step, children go quiet and watch. They see that beauty takes effort and a bit of courage, and that’s a lesson in patience in its own right.

Main lesson books echo the board

Afterwards children often draw part of the board picture, or their own version, in their books with beeswax crayons or coloured pencils. The board is the starting point and the book is the child’s own take on it.

III.Chalk and pastels teachers and parents can use

Stockmar block chalk is the usual choice in Waldorf classrooms. These are other options that work as board chalk, whether you teach or you’re drawing at the kitchen table.

Made in the Netherlands

Mercurius blackboard chalk

The Mercurius “Art Makes Sense” blackboard pastel chalk is made with natural colour pigments only, with no synthetic pigments added. It’s soft enough for drawing without being brittle and strong enough to write with without scratching. One Waldorf shop says it’s their favourite chalk for low dust that cleans up easily. It comes in assorted sets, such as 16 colours.

Mercurius also makes an anti-dust hard chalk in 8 colours, which is good for writing when you don’t want chalk on your fingers. It’s a strong chalk, so it can leave a residue on some boards. A damp cloth gets it off.

Also from Mercurius: a felt eraser block that cleans the board without water. Never wash it, just beat the chalk out.

Artist pastels

Rembrandt soft pastels

Rembrandt pastels (Royal Talens, made in the Netherlands) are artist-grade soft pastels with kaolin clay as the binder. They’re rich in pigment, free from lead, cadmium and cobalt, and rated lightfast. They’re medium soft, so they lay colour down easily but still hold a sharp line and an edge. The range is large: 42 pure colours plus lighter and darker shades of each. Sold as full or half sticks, with the half sticks cheaper.

They aren’t made for blackboards, so try one on your own board before you buy a big set. Expect more colour and a bit more cost than regular board chalk. A small set of half sticks is a good way to start.

TipBefore you use a new chalkboard, rub a piece of chalk on its side over the whole surface, then wipe it off with a dry cloth. This seasons the board so colour goes on evenly.

IV.Teachers and artists to follow

⭐ Netherlands / USA

Arne Kaiser — My Chalkboard

Born in Amsterdam and went to Waldorf school in Bergen, Norway from age 10. He taught at the Geert Groote School in Amsterdam, then at Pine Forest School in Flagstaff, Arizona from 2002. His book My Chalkboard walks through Grades 1–8 with almost 400 chalk drawings from real lessons, plus practical advice for each grade. He has also written Personal Verses (60 birthday and personal verses for children) and The Little Red Hen, a Grade 1 play for 10–40 children.

Book on blurb.com and Amazon · pinterest.com/arnekaiser

⭐ USA

Narsingh Khalsa — Waldorf Art & Teacher

A Waldorf teacher and a prolific chalkboard artist. His Facebook page has over 49,000 followers, and his Instagram and Pinterest boards cover Grades 1–8. His Etsy shop, TeachingWaldorf, sells main lesson book pages, drawing guides and planning tools.

@khalsaart · Facebook: Waldorf Art and Teacher · Etsy: TeachingWaldorf

⭐ USA

Wondergarten — Nathaniel & Lisa Santa Cruz

A home preschool in Puyallup, Washington, that started in 2022 and went viral. Lisa wrote the Wondergarten Curriculum (2024), a seasonal early childhood program of stories, songs and chalkboard drawings. Nathaniel, a former Marine who later worked on Google Maps, writes the songs and stories and draws the chalkboard art. They also sell a music theory chalkboard print and run a community on Mighty Networks.

wondergarten.net · @wondergarten

USA

Steve Simonak

A 5th Grade teacher at Brooklyn Waldorf School, previously 26 years at Hawthorne Valley. He has a BFA in painting from Pratt. Each July he teaches a weekend workshop at Sunbridge Institute in Chestnut Ridge, NY, on chalkboards and main lesson books.

USA

Gail Lescher — Prairie Moon video workshops

A Waldorf master teacher. Prairie Moon Waldorf School produced two videos with her: a 100-minute Chalk Board Drawing Lesson for all levels, and a full-day Advanced Chalkboard Drawings workshop of 4+ hours.

prairiemoon.org/training-videos · videos@prairiemoon.org

Australia

Waldorfish — Robyn Beaufoy & Brian Wolfe

Trained Waldorf teachers with 25+ years in art and education. Their online courses include Waldorf Art for Beginners, which covers chalk basics, a full chalk landscape lesson and how to make your own chalkboard. They also run Weekly Art Foundations and a 32-lesson course for high school age. There’s a free chalkboard gallery on the site.

waldorfish.com

USA · Substack

Meredith Floyd-Preston — Waldorf at Work

A Waldorf class teacher who writes about classroom art, board drawing tips, hand lettering and main lesson book titles, with step-by-step videos. She writes as a non-artist who built her skills over the years, so it’s honest and practical.

waldorfatwork.substack.com
