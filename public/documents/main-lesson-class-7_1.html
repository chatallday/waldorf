Class 7 — The Age of Discovery

The seventh-grader is a discoverer themselves — of their own thinking, their own body, their own opinions

I.The Year at a Glance

Inner gesture: Everything is opening. The world is bigger than I thought. I want to think for myself.

Class 7 is the year of discovery — outwardly and inwardly. The young adolescent is discovering their own mind, their own body, their own opinions, and the curriculum mirrors this with the European Age of Discovery: the voyages of Columbus and Magellan, the Renaissance opening of the imagination, the Reformation breaking of old certainties, the rise of science. At the same time, the children meet their own bodies in the physiology block — often the most fascinating block of the year. Algebra arrives, taking the great leap into abstract thinking. Chemistry begins. And in the famous "Wish, Wonder, and Surprise" block, the children's own restless inner life is met with creative writing.

II.The Main Lesson Blocks

These are the traditional Class 7 main lesson blocks. Schools vary in exactly which they teach and when, but the underlying pattern is consistent worldwide.

The Renaissance and Reformation

1–2 blocks · 3–4 weeks each

The flowering of Europe out of the Middle Ages — Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, Raphael, Galileo, Copernicus. The Reformation and Luther's break with the old church. The printing press. The opening of the mind to its own powers. The block is full of biographies of remarkable people who thought for themselves — exactly what the seventh-grader is beginning to do.

The Age of Exploration

1 block · 3–4 weeks

The European voyages — Columbus, Vasco da Gama, Magellan, Drake. The opening of the seas, the meeting of continents, the dark and light of colonisation. Maps now show the whole earth; the children draw the lines of the great voyages across the oceans they study.

Physiology

1 block · 3–4 weeks

The human body in detail — the threefold human being (head, rhythmic system, metabolic system), the heart and the circulation, the lungs and breathing, the digestion, the muscles, the senses. The block is met by adolescents with fascination — they are, after all, in the middle of becoming new bodies themselves. Beautifully illustrated work fills the main lesson book.

Inorganic Chemistry

1 block · 3–4 weeks

The first chemistry block. Combustion (the candle, the campfire), acids and bases, salts, the chemistry of lime and sulphur and salt. Demonstrations are real — flames, foaming, colour changes. The block is taught phenomenologically: the children see, then describe, then understand.

Algebra

1–2 blocks · 3–4 weeks each

The great leap into abstract thinking. Variables — letters standing for unknown quantities. Equations to be solved. The transition from arithmetic to algebra is one of the most important academic moments of the Waldorf curriculum, and Class 7 is its threshold.

Geography of Africa and Asia

1 block

After the Americas in earlier grades, the children meet Africa and Asia — the Sahara, the Nile, the Himalayas, the great rivers of India and China, the steppes of central Asia. The geography of the whole earth slowly comes into view.

"Wish, Wonder, and Surprise" — Creative Writing

1 block

A famous Waldorf Class 7 block. The students write creative pieces around three soul-states — wish (longing for something), wonder (astonishment at the world), and surprise (the unexpected). The block meets the inner restlessness and yearning of the young adolescent and gives them a place to put it.

III.A Sample Weekly Rhythm

Within each block, the days of the week each have their own gentle character. Here is a sample rhythm a Class 7 teacher might keep.

A typical week inside a Class 7 block

  • Monday New material in the current block — biography, story, phenomenon
  • Tuesday Recall, writing, illustration; the main lesson book takes shape
  • Wednesday Algebra day — equations, problem-solving, abstract reasoning
  • Thursday Practical observation — chemistry demonstration, physiology observation, geography mapping
  • Friday Composition, creative writing, the week reviewed

IV.Main Lesson Book — Example Pages

Each block produces its own hand-made book. Here are examples of what Class 7 children might draw and write into their main lesson books across the year.

  • A portrait of Leonardo da Vinci, with his Vitruvian Man reconstructed in the borders using compass and straightedge.
  • A diagram of the human heart, the four chambers carefully drawn and labelled, the great vessels in red and blue.
  • Magellan's route around the world, drawn across a world map, with the duration of each leg of the voyage noted.
  • A page on the candle — the wax melting, the wick burning, the flame's structure (yellow zone, dark inner cone, blue base) — with observations and the chemistry of combustion.
  • An algebraic equation solved beautifully, with each step in a different colour.

V.The Year's Morning Verse

Each Waldorf main lesson opens with a verse. In Classes 1–4 the children speak the verse for the younger grades, written by Rudolf Steiner; from Class 5 onward they speak the upper-grades verse.

I look into the world
In which the sun is shining,
In which the stars are sparkling,
In which the stones repose... — The upper-grades morning verse continues

A small library of resources for teachers, homeschoolers, and curious parents.

  • Manfred von Mackensen — A Phenomena-Based Physics, Vol. 2 — For the Class 7 physics extension
  • Waldorf Chemistry Curriculum books — Look for the Frits Julius works and the AWSNA chemistry curriculum
  • Roy Wilkinson — The Human Being — A useful guide for the physiology block
  • Jamie York — Making Math Meaningful: Grade 7 Sourcebook — For Class 7 algebra
