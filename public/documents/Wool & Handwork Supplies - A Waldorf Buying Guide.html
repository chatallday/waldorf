I.Why wool matters

Traditional Waldorf handwork uses natural wool only, never synthetic.

Wool breathes, keeps you warm without overheating, and is naturally antibacterial and full of lanolin. Waldorf teachers also value it for how it works on the child: knitting builds concentration and coordination between both hands, and felting builds spatial sense and fine motor control. Stick to natural fibres, and plant dyes where you can.

The three main wool forms

  • 🧶 Yarn — spun for knitting, crochet, weaving and fingerknitting
  • 🌀 Roving / loose wool — carded or combed fibres for spinning, stuffing, wet and needle felting
  • 🟩 Felt sheets — pressed wool for cutting, sewing, crowns, figures, ornaments and appliqué

III.Filges, the wool most schools use

Filges is the Waldorf wool brand you'll find in schools almost everywhere. It's a family business founded in Germany in 1985 as a small wool spinning and combing works, it later added a plant-colour dyeing mill. It has been Bioland-certified, the highest organic standard in Germany, since 1995. The wool comes only from certified organic German sheep farms, and all dyeing uses non-toxic plant dyes with alum and tartar as mordants.
