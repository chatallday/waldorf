Class 4 — The World of Forces and Choices

A newly independent child meets bold, energetic, morally complex tales

I.The Year at a Glance

Inner gesture: Forces meet and shape the world. Even gods make choices. I am one being among many.

Having crossed the nine-year change, the Class 4 child emerges newly independent — sometimes argumentative, sometimes obstinate, full of energy and questions. They are no longer satisfied with simple stories of good and evil. They need myths in which gods quarrel, heroes fail, tricksters scheme, and even the immortal must eventually face an ending. Norse mythology, with its bold, physical, morally complex tales, meets this child exactly. Academically, Class 4 also turns outward — the children study their local geography in real depth, draw their first proper maps, and meet the animal kingdom as a mirror in which the human form is seen more clearly. Math takes the great leap into fractions; grammar grows into composition.

II.The Main Lesson Blocks

These are the traditional Class 4 main lesson blocks. Schools vary in exactly which they teach and when, but the underlying pattern is consistent worldwide.

Norse Mythology

1–2 blocks · 3–4 weeks each

The myths of the Norse — Odin trading an eye for wisdom, Thor and his thunderous hammer, Loki the trickster, the binding of Fenrir the wolf, the building of Asgard's wall, the death of beautiful Baldur, the long approach of Ragnarök. The stories are vivid, physical, sometimes funny, sometimes tragic. They give the child a picture of a world where great forces meet and shape each other, where even gods must choose.

The Human Being and the Animal

1 block · 3–4 weeks

The first proper natural science block. The animal kingdom is studied not as a collection of species but in three great groupings — head-animals (cuttlefish, octopus), trunk-animals (lions, cows, horses), and limb-animals (eagles, deer). The child sees how the animal is one-sided where the human being is balanced, and how the upright human form unites in itself qualities scattered across the animal world. A truly beautiful Waldorf block.

Local Geography

1 block · 3–4 weeks

The child's own region drawn on a hand-made map. They begin in their classroom, draw it. Then the school grounds. Then the neighbourhood. Then the town, the river, the mountains. Geography starts where the child stands and works outward. Maps are made by hand, with care.

Mathematics — Fractions

2 blocks across the year

Fractions introduced as real-world divisions — cutting a loaf into halves and quarters, sharing apples among three friends, measuring fabric. Equivalent fractions, addition and subtraction of fractions, are met through cooking, building, and proportion before they are met on paper.

Mathematics — Long Division Mastery, Factoring

1–2 blocks

Long division consolidated and made fluent. Factoring numbers — discovering the building blocks of each number. Mental math becomes a daily practice.

Grammar and Composition

runs through the year

The parts of speech in real depth; the tenses (past, present, future); the first paragraph-length compositions. Children begin to write substantial passages about what they have learned.

Form Drawing — Celtic Knots and Braids

weekly through the year

Form drawing matures into intricate Celtic knotwork, braided forms, interweaving patterns. The hand becomes capable of true precision. These forms also connect beautifully to the Norse and Celtic story material.

III.A Sample Weekly Rhythm

Within each block, the days of the week each have their own gentle character. Here is a sample rhythm a Class 4 teacher might keep.

A typical week inside a Class 4 block

  • Monday New Norse myth told; the children meet the gods of the week
  • Tuesday Recall, draw, write in the main lesson book; mental math
  • Wednesday Mathematics block focus — fractions, division
  • Thursday Geography or natural science block focus; map work
  • Friday Form drawing or composition; the week is gathered and reflected

IV.Main Lesson Book — Example Pages

Each block produces its own hand-made book. Here are examples of what Class 4 children might draw and write into their main lesson books across the year.

  • Thor wielding Mjölnir against the giants, the hammer drawn in red against a stormy grey sky; the story of how the hammer was forged.
  • A diagram of the cuttlefish alongside the human being, showing how the cuttlefish is "all head" while the human being is balanced.
  • A hand-drawn map of the school grounds with the four directions, the building, the play area, and the trees — drawn from memory and observation.
  • A Celtic knot border in red and blue around the page-opening for a new section of the Norse myths.
  • A page of equivalent fractions, shown both as numbers and as cut-up circles in colour.

V.The Year's Morning Verse

Each Waldorf main lesson opens with a verse. In Classes 1–4 the children speak the verse for the younger grades, written by Rudolf Steiner; from Class 5 onward they speak the upper-grades verse.

The sun with loving light
makes bright for me each day.
The soul with spirit power
gives strength unto my limbs... — The Classes 1–4 morning verse, in its final year

A small library of resources for teachers, homeschoolers, and curious parents.

  • Charles Kovacs — The Human Being and the Animal World — For the animal block in Class 4

  • Eric Fairman — A Path of Discovery, Vol. 4 — Waldorf curriculum guide for Class 4
