I.The Year at a Glance

Inner gesture: The world is lawful. There are causes and consequences. I want to know the structure of things.

Class 6 marks the threshold of adolescence. The body is changing — sometimes dramatically. The dreamy Greek balance of Class 5 gives way to a harder, more lawful inner orientation. The child wants structure, law, hierarchy, cause and effect. They want to know how things actually work. The Waldorf curriculum meets this with extraordinary precision: Rome with its laws and roads; the Middle Ages with their guilds and codes of honour; the rocks and minerals of the earth (the most lawful kingdom of nature); the first proper geometry with compass and straightedge; and the first physics, where phenomena are met phenomenologically and the laws behind them slowly emerge. Class 6 is a turning point: childhood ends, adolescence begins, and the Waldorf curriculum walks the child across that threshold.

II.The Main Lesson Blocks

These are the traditional Class 6 main lesson blocks. Schools vary in exactly which they teach and when, but the underlying pattern is consistent worldwide.

Roman History

1–2 blocks · 3–4 weeks each

From the founding of Rome (Romulus and Remus, the she-wolf) through the Republic, the great figures (Cincinnatus, the Gracchi, Caesar), the Empire, the Pax Romana, and finally the decline. Roman law, Roman roads, the army, the language. The child meets the world's first great civilisation built explicitly on law rather than on myth.

The Middle Ages

1 block

Following Rome's fall — the early medieval world, monasteries, the knights and ladies, the guilds, the Crusades, the cathedrals, the troubadours. Stories of King Arthur, Charlemagne, Saint Francis. A world of orders, codes, hierarchies — exactly what the Class 6 mind is hungry for.

Geometry

1 block · 3–4 weeks

The first formal geometry, with compass and straightedge. The child constructs perfect circles, equilateral triangles, hexagons, the six-petalled rose. They discover the regularities of these forms with their own hands. This is one of the great Class 6 blocks — geometric beauty meets adolescent precision.

Physics

1 block · 3–4 weeks

The first physics, taught phenomenologically — that is, the children observe phenomena first and discover the laws afterwards. Acoustics (the singing of strings, the resonance of tubes); optics (light through prisms, the camera obscura); heat (expansion, conduction); magnetism; static electricity. Each phenomenon is met fresh, then drawn and written about, then a law gradually emerges.

Mineralogy / Earth Science

1 block · 3–4 weeks

The rocks and crystals of the earth — the most lawful, most ordered kingdom of nature. Crystal forms and their geometric perfection, the great mountain ranges, volcanoes and earthquakes, the layers of the earth. Field trips to quarries or rock outcrops; specimens passed around in the classroom.

South American Geography

1 block

The continent of South America — its great river systems (Amazon, Orinoco), the Andes, the rainforest, the ancient civilisations (Incas, the great cities of pre-Columbian times). Maps continue to grow more substantial and detailed.

Mathematics — Business Math

1–2 blocks

Percentages, profit and loss, interest, the calculations of practical commerce. Often connected to the Roman block (the Roman economy) and to real-world life skills.

III.A Sample Weekly Rhythm

Within each block, the days of the week each have their own gentle character. Here is a sample rhythm a Class 6 teacher might keep.

A typical week inside a Class 6 block

  • Monday Roman or medieval story; introduction of new material
  • Tuesday Recall and writing; main lesson book work; mental math
  • Wednesday Geometry day — compass and straightedge construction
  • Thursday Physics — a new phenomenon observed, drawn, discussed
  • Friday Review the week; geography or business math; closing

IV.Main Lesson Book — Example Pages

Each block produces its own hand-made book. Here are examples of what Class 6 children might draw and write into their main lesson books across the year.

  • Caesar crossing the Rubicon, in red ink against the river; the text of his words "the die is cast" underneath.
  • A perfectly drawn six-petalled rose inside a circle, with the construction lines preserved in light pencil and the final figure in red.
  • A page on the camera obscura — a careful drawing of the box, the pinhole, the inverted image on the back wall — and the principle of light travelling in straight lines.
  • A specimen page of quartz, calcite, and pyrite, each drawn in fine detail with its crystal form, its hardness, and where it is found.
  • A map of the Roman Empire at its greatest extent, with the roads marked, the provinces labelled, and the major cities placed.

V.The Year's Morning Verse

Each Waldorf main lesson opens with a verse. In Classes 1–4 the children speak the verse for the younger grades, written by Rudolf Steiner; from Class 5 onward they speak the upper-grades verse.

I look into the world
In which the sun is shining,
In which the stars are sparkling,
In which the stones repose... — The upper-grades morning verse continues

VI.Recommended Books

A small library of resources for teachers, homeschoolers, and curious parents.

  • Charles Kovacs — Ancient Rome — Waldorf retelling for the Rome block
  • Charles Kovacs — The Age of Revolution and Roman and Medieval History — For the medieval block
  • Charles Kovacs — Geometry — Class 6 geometry curriculum
  • Eric Fairman — A Path of Discovery, Vol. 6 — Waldorf curriculum guide for Class 6
  • Manfred von Mackensen — A Phenomena-Based Physics, Vol. 1 — The classic Waldorf approach to teaching physics
  • Charles Kovacs — Minerals, Plants, Animals — For the mineralogy block
  • Hawthorne — Tanglewood Tales and similar collections — For Greek and Roman myth material
