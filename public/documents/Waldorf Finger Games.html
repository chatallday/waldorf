I.What finger games are

A short verse or song, a few hand movements, and an adult who is paying attention.

You may also hear them called finger plays, hand gesture games or gesture plays. People have played them with small children in every culture we know of, and they are some of the oldest things we do with young children.

In Waldorf early childhood work they are not there to fill a gap in the morning. Steiner is quoted as saying that nimble fingers become nimble thinking later in life, and Waldorf teachers take that seriously. The games are part of how a child gets ready for reading, writing, number work and getting along with other people.

Wilma Ellersiek (1921–2007) taught music, speech and dance at the Stuttgart State Academy for Music and Performing Arts. In her later years she studied what rhythm, movement and language do for a young child, and the gesture games she wrote came out of that work. Most Waldorf kindergartens that use gesture games today are using hers.

II.Why they matter

Ages 2–9

Small hands get strong

A finger that can point, tap, weave and gesture on purpose is closer to holding a pencil, a knitting needle or a bow. The muscles are the same ones.

Ages 0–7

Both sides of the body work together

Many games use both hands at once, or pass a movement from the left hand to the right and across the middle of the body. That crossing is the same coordination a child will need later to read and write.

All ages

Words stay when the body helps

Rhyme, repetition and movement together are very good for learning language. The child who has climbed a spider up the drainpipe with their fingers is not going to forget the word drainpipe.

Ages 2–8

A steady beat

Keeping a beat with the fingers while speaking a verse trains a child to hold time inside. Music, maths and playing along with other people all lean on that.

Ages 3–7

Numbers you can feel

"One little finger, two little fingers" teaches counting before anyone mentions arithmetic. The numbers are right there at the end of the child's arms.

Birth–age 5

Being close

For the youngest children this matters most. An adult sits down, sings, touches a hand and has no other place to be. Ellersiek's Giving Love, Bringing Joy is built around touch games between parent and baby for this reason.

III.How to share one with a child

  • Learn the verse well enough that you can play it without looking at the page.
  • Do the whole game yourself, slowly and with your full attention. The child only watches.
  • Do it again. Children join in when they are ready, and some take weeks. Don't ask them to show you.
  • Play the same verse every day for a good while. The repeating is the point. It is how the words and the movement settle into the body.
  • Keep your voice warm and plain. A big performance voice and big gestures get in the way.
  • Say it clearly and let the rhythm do the work. The child's hands will follow.
If you feel awkwardAlmost everyone does at first. Pick one short verse, say it out loud a few times in the car or the kitchen, and try it on your child when you have a quiet few minutes. It gets easier quickly.

IV.The Ellersiek books

All published by WECAN and translated by Lyn and Kundry Willwerth. Each one has a companion CD. They are available from waldorfearlychildhood.org/store.

⭐ Start here

Giving Love, Bringing Joy

Hand gesture games and lullabies in the Mood of the Fifth (WECAN, 2003). For the youngest children, from birth on. It has touch games for parent and baby, lullabies, and games for lying down. The book describes its aim as helping children build a caring relationship with plants, animals, people and things through loving contact.

ISBN 978-0-9796232-6-4

⭐ Autumn and winter

Gesture Games for Autumn and Winter

32 songs and gesture games for kindergarten and the lower grades, with autumn and winter nature themes. Margret Costanini's introduction explains the thinking behind teaching by example and imitation.

ISBN 978-0-9722238-9-8

⭐ Spring and summer

Gesture Games for Spring and Summer

32 more, this time from spring and summer (WECAN, 2005). It includes craft directions and a short biography of Ellersiek. You can buy it together with the autumn and winter book as a discounted set with CD.

ISBN 978-0-9722238-0-5

⭐ Moving more

Dancing Hand, Trotting Pony

Finger plays plus bigger movement games, including animal movements and circle games. Suits kindergarten through the lower grades.

⭐ The last volume

Gnomes and Giants, Pixies and Elves

Ellersiek did not finish this one. Her colleagues completed it after her death, including Lynn St Pierre, who trained with Kundry Willwerth in Stuttgart (WECAN, 2014). It is full of imaginative gesture play about gnomes, giants, pixies and elves.

Buying tipThere is a four-book set with Giving Love Bringing Joy, both Gesture Games books and Dancing Hand Trotting Pony. If you work with young children, or you plan to use these for years at home, the set costs less than buying them one at a time.

V.Other collections

Waldorf Publications

Finger Plays

56 finger games for children from about 3, compiled by Mercury Press, with the verses and the finger movements printed side by side. It was the top seller on the Waldorf Publications list as of 2024. It is cheap, easy to use and a good first book.

WECAN

A Lifetime of Joy, by Bronja Zahlingen

Circle games, finger games, songs, verses and puppet plays in one book. A lot of Waldorf kindergartens around the world keep a copy.

VI.Trainers and workshops

⭐ USA / international

Lynn St Pierre

Works in the Ellersiek tradition and is also a Spacial Dynamics teacher trainer. She apprenticed with Kundry Willwerth and was a member of the Stuttgart Hand Gesture Working Circle. She co-wrote Gnomes and Giants, Pixies and Elves and teaches touch, hand gesture and large movement play at Waldorf teacher training centres, conferences and schools. She is on the faculty of Dancing Hands & Frolicking Voices, a two-year intensive run each year with Laurie Clark.

dancinghandsfrolickingvoices.org

⭐ Chestnut Ridge, New York

Sunbridge Institute: Movement and Singing in the Ellersiek Tradition

A summer course taught by Lynn St Pierre. You spend the time doing finger plays, circle games, singing in the Mood of the Fifth and speaking verse, and you go home able to use it with children right away.

sunbridge.edu/event/movement-singing-ellersiek-tradition

Remembered

Kundry Willwerth (1932–2017)

A Waldorf kindergarten teacher for more than 30 years, and one of the founders of the Stuttgart Hand Gesture Working Circle. She translated and edited the Ellersiek books into English and taught workshops in North America, Europe, Brazil and Australia. Hundreds of teachers have trained with her or with people she trained.

VII.Free resources online

  • Lavender's Blue Homeschool: seasonal finger games on video, with clear notes on what each one does for a child. lavendersbluehomeschool.com/blogs/magic-fingerplays
  • Soul Blossom Kids: Waldorf-inspired finger plays and hand games with videos, including spring and summer fairy games. soulblossomkids.com
  • tiptoes-lightly.net: Reg Down's site has dozens of finger verses, action verses and seasonal poems. One of the fullest free Waldorf early years sites around.
  • Cedarwood Waldorf School: a free collection of fingerplays and gesture verses sorted by season. cedarwoodschool.org/buttercup/song-verse
  • YouTube: many Waldorf teachers post their finger games. Try searching "Waldorf finger plays", "Waldorf gesture games" or "Ellersiek finger games". The Lambkin and Fairy fingerplay videos are good ones to start with and come with teaching notes.

VIII.A few verses to start with

These are short and I wrote them for this guide. Change the words to suit your own place and season.

Autumn

The Falling Leaf

Little leaf up on the bough,
Wiggle, wiggle, wiggle now.
Wind comes whispering, soft and low,
Down, down, down you go.
Rest upon the earth so deep,
Sleep, little leaf, and softly sleep. Hold a hand high with the fingers spread. Wiggle them, sway, then flutter the hand down into the other palm and cover it.
Winter

The Little Candle

One small candle, bright and still,
Warms the winter, warms the hill.
Two small hands curve round the light,
Keep it safe through the long night. One finger up as the flame. Sway it gently, then cup both hands around it.
Spring

The Waking Seed

Seed lies sleeping in the ground,
Not a sound, not a sound.
Rain comes tapping, pitter-pat,
Sun shines warm, and then, and that...
Up comes one green finger tall,
Then a leaf, and then they all! Start with a closed fist in your lap. Tap it with the other hand's fingers, then let one finger rise slowly and open the whole hand like a flower.
Summer

The Butterfly

Butterfly, butterfly, wings of gold,
Dancing in the sun's warm hold.
Over the daisies, over the bee,
Over the buttercups, over me.
Landing softly on my knee,
Butterfly, butterfly, can you see me? Cross the wrists, link the thumbs and flutter the fingers slowly. Float from side to side, then rest on the knee.
For babies

Little Fingers Walking

Little feet walk, walk, walk,
Up the arm and round the neck,
Tickle, tickle, one, two, three,
Little baby, you and me. Walk two fingers up the baby's arm and tickle softly under the chin. Stop if the baby turns away.
