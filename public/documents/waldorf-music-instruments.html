Waldorf Music and Instruments

"The human being is a musical being, and the making of music is essential in experiencing what it is to be fully human." — Rudolf Steiner

In a Waldorf school, music isn't a separate subject. It runs through the whole day. A child who grows up in a home where people sing, play instruments and mark the day with rhythm is already well fed musically by the time school starts.

You don't need a Waldorf school to do this. Most of it can be done at home.

II.Bringing it home

Start small. Singing costs nothing.

All families · Ages 0+

Start with the kinder lyre

The kinder lyre (also called a kinder harp or pentatonic lyre) is the most useful Waldorf instrument to have at home. It's tuned to the pentatonic scale, so whatever you pluck sounds fine, and you don't need to know music. Play it quietly at bedtime or rest time, or to go with singing. Kids from about 2 can pluck it. It's the instrument most often recommended first. You can get one from Choroi, Auris, Harps of Lorien and many Waldorf suppliers.

All ages · All families

Sing every day

The most important thing you can do is sing, even if you don't like your voice. Children don't hear the flaws. They hear that you're there and you care. Sing the songs for moving between activities. Sing the seasonal songs, the meal blessing and a lullaby. Your voice is good enough.

Ages 6+ · Grade 1

The pentatonic flute

A Choroi pentatonic flute is the first instrument a Grade 1 child gets. It has five notes and they all go together. Kids learn by copying, not by reading music. At home, you can learn alongside your child. Simple seasonal songs, lullabies and nature tunes are doable within the first few lessons. Available from Choroi.org and Waldorf instrument suppliers, roughly €25 to €35.

Ages 2+ · Kindergarten

Pentatonic glockenspiel

The Auris pentatonic glockenspiel is a favorite home instrument. It has six or eight bars in a wooden frame and is played with a soft mallet. A child can play it alone and never hit a wrong note. Good for toddlers exploring sound and for free play. Available from Auris (aurisinstruments.com), Bella Luna Toys and Waldorf suppliers worldwide.

Ages 9+ · Grade 3

Recorder

The soprano recorder is the standard instrument for Grades 3 to 8. It's cheap, easy to carry and sounds lovely when played well. Mollenhauer and Moeck are the German makers of wooden recorders that Waldorf schools prefer. If you can, skip plastic. Wood has a warmer tone. A decent wooden soprano recorder runs about €20 to €40. For lessons at home, look for the Waldorf recorder songbook series from various publishers.

Ages 9+ · Grade 3

Strings

Most Waldorf schools start violin or cello in Grade 3. Renting for the first year or two is strongly recommended over buying. String teaching in Waldorf is mostly by ear and imitation. The Suzuki method fits with this and is widely used alongside it. In the early years, 10 to 15 minutes of practice every day beats an occasional long session.

Ages 2 to 10 · Home circle

Singing games and circle time

For young kids, the singing game is about the simplest and most complete music practice there is. Movement, song, rhythm and playing together all happen at once. Three books to look for, all from WECAN: Singing Games for Families, Schools and Communities (Anna Rainville and Laurie Clark), Dancing As We Sing (Nancy Foster) and Let Us Form a Ring (Nancy Foster). Between them they cover what a family needs.

USA

Harps of Lorien: kinder lyres

Raphael Weisman has made kinder lyres for over 20 years. He's one of the most trusted North American makers of pentatonic children's lyres, and his instruments are used with the Christopherus Waldorf homeschool curriculum. Good quality at a fair price.

harpsoflorien.com

waldorfmusic.org
YouTube

Choroi's channel shows all their instruments being played by teachers and children: flutes, lyres and glockenspiels. A good way to hear an instrument before you buy it. Search "Choroi Waldorf music."

YouTube

Waldorf circle songs and pentatonic songs

Lots of Waldorf teachers post circle time songs, lyre playing and seasonal music. Try searching "Waldorf circle time songs," "Waldorf pentatonic lyre" or "Waldorf kindergarten music." Sarah Baldwin (Sunday with Sarah, WECAN) is a good one to start with, along with various school music teachers.

IV.Instrument makers

Who makes what.

Germany

Choroi

The best-known Waldorf instrument maker. Developed with the Goetheanum Medical Section. Their workshops employ disabled and disadvantaged workers. Pentatonic flutes in pear or cherry wood (440 Hz and 432 Hz), plus lyres, harps and glockenspiels.

choroi.org
Sweden

Auris

Pentatonic and diatonic glockenspiels, xylophones, and children's harps and lyres, in maple and birch. Widely used in Waldorf schools.

Germany

Mollenhauer

High-quality wooden recorders in soprano, alto and tenor. Popular in the upper grades.

Germany

Moeck

Wooden recorders, used from Grade 3 on.

Germany

Hokema

Kalimbas in natural cherry wood, in pentatonic tunings (B5, B9, B17).

Germany

Bartl

Harmonicas, widely used in the lower grades.
