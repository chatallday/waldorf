I.The Year at a Glance

Inner gesture: I am becoming an adult. I can take on real work, follow it through, and present it to the world.

Class 8 is the great closing chapter of the Waldorf grade-school journey. The student has nearly finished their time with the class teacher who has walked alongside them for eight years. They stand at the door of high school — and of adulthood. The curriculum honours this moment with serious, substantial work: modern history (the Industrial Revolution through the twentieth century), organic chemistry (the chemistry of life), physiology and anatomy, advanced algebra and geometry, the famous Class 8 play (usually a full Shakespeare), and the Class 8 Project — a substantial independent research project the student designs, researches, executes, and presents publicly. By the end of Class 8, the child of Class 1 has become a young person ready to take on the demands of high school.

II.The Main Lesson Blocks

These are the traditional Class 8 main lesson blocks. Schools vary in exactly which they teach and when, but the underlying pattern is consistent worldwide.

Modern History

2 blocks · 3–4 weeks each

From the Industrial Revolution to the twentieth century — the rise of the modern world. Biographies of decisive figures: Watt and the steam engine, the abolitionists, Lincoln, Gandhi, the rise of democracy, the world wars, the civil rights movement. The block is rich, demanding, and meets the eighth-grader's growing sense of justice and historical awareness.

Organic Chemistry

1 block · 3–4 weeks

The chemistry of life — sugars, starches, proteins, fats and oils, alcohols. Practical experiments: fermentation, the testing of sugars, the production of soap. Connected closely to the physiology of human nutrition. A culminating chemistry block.

Physics — Hydraulics, Aerodynamics, Electromagnetism

1 block · 3–4 weeks

Forces and machines that shape the modern world — hydraulic systems, aerodynamics (flight), meteorology, and the deep meeting of electricity and magnetism (Faraday, Maxwell, the motor and generator). The block prepares the foundation for high school physics.

Anatomy

1 block · 3–4 weeks

The bones, muscles, joints, and senses of the human body, in real anatomical depth. Skeletal drawings, the workings of the eye and ear, the lever-systems of the joints. A serious, substantial study that complements the Class 7 physiology block.

Advanced Algebra and Geometry

runs through the year

Algebra deepened — quadratics, factoring, systems of equations. Geometry: the Pythagorean theorem proved and used, the platonic solids constructed and studied, the beginnings of trigonometry. Mathematics is now seriously academic, and the children are ready.

World Geography

1 block

The whole earth now. Climate zones, ocean currents, the cultures of the world, global economics, environmental questions. The geography block closes the grade-school journey through the earth that began with the local map in Class 4.

The Class 8 Play

rehearsed across several weeks, performed publicly

Usually a full Shakespeare production — A Midsummer Night's Dream, The Tempest, Twelfth Night. Performed over several evenings on a proper stage with full costumes, lights, and live music. The crown of the entire grade-school drama programme. (See the Class Play guide in the directory for more.)

The Class 8 Project

individual; runs across many months · presented publicly

Each student designs and executes a substantial independent research project, usually presented publicly to the school at the end of the year. Projects range widely — building a kayak, learning to make traditional bread, mastering a musical instrument, studying a historical figure, learning to sew a whole garment. The Class 8 Project is the student's first piece of truly self-directed adult-style work, and it is one of the great rites of passage of Waldorf education.

III.A Sample Weekly Rhythm

Within each block, the days of the week each have their own gentle character. Here is a sample rhythm a Class 8 teacher might keep.

A typical week inside a Class 8 block

  • Monday New material in the current block — substantial, demanding
  • Tuesday Recall, in-depth writing, mathematical work
  • Wednesday Practical observation or experiment; main lesson book deepens
  • Thursday Algebra or geometry — formal mathematical work
  • Friday Composition, presentation practice, project work; closing

IV.Main Lesson Book — Example Pages

Each block produces its own hand-made book. Here are examples of what Class 8 children might draw and write into their main lesson books across the year.

  • A page on the steam engine — Watt's design carefully drawn, the principle of pressure and condensation explained.
  • A skeletal drawing of the human hand, each bone labelled in Latin, the small joints of the fingers shown clearly.
  • An organic chemistry page on fermentation — the chemical equation, a diagram of the apparatus, observations from the demonstration.
  • A Pythagorean theorem proof, drawn geometrically, with squares constructed on each side of the right triangle and shown to be equal.
  • A timeline of the twentieth century, with the major events placed against a horizontal axis and biographical portraits of key figures.

V.The Year's Morning Verse

Each Waldorf main lesson opens with a verse. In Classes 1–4 the children speak the verse for the younger grades, written by Rudolf Steiner; from Class 5 onward they speak the upper-grades verse.

I look into the world
In which the sun is shining,
In which the stars are sparkling,
In which the stones repose;
Where living plants are growing,
Where sentient beasts are living,
Where man within his soul
Gives dwelling to the spirit;
I look into the soul
Which dwells within myself... — The upper-grades morning verse, in its final year before high school

VI.Recommended Books

A small library of resources for teachers, homeschoolers, and curious parents.

  • Charles Kovacs — The Age of Revolution — Useful for the modern history block
  • Eric Fairman — A Path of Discovery, Vol. 8 — Waldorf curriculum guide for Class 8
  • Manfred von Mackensen — A Phenomena-Based Physics, Vol. 3 — For Class 8 physics — hydraulics, aerodynamics, electromagnetism
  • Henning Köhler — Working with Anxious, Nervous and Depressed Children — Useful background for working with the inner life of the adolescent
  • Jamie York — Making Math Meaningful: Grade 8 Sourcebook — For Class 8 mathematics
  • A good Shakespeare edition — Folger or Arden series — For the Class 8 play
  • Roy Wilkinson — The Human Being and the Living Earth — Useful guide for the anatomy and physiology blocks
