Class 2 — The Two Sides of Human Nature

The contrast between human weakness (fables) and human greatness (saint stories)

I.The Year at a Glance

Inner gesture: I see myself in both the fox and the saint. I can choose which I become.

By Class 2, the child has crossed into the second seven-year period and is beginning to sense their own inner contradictions — that they can be both selfish and generous, kind and unkind, sometimes in the same hour. The Class 2 curriculum meets this awakening with a brilliant pairing: fables (which show human weaknesses in animal form, often comically) and saint stories (which show the very highest possibilities of the human being). The two together let the child meet both sides of human nature without judgement and without overwhelm. Academic work consolidates and deepens. Math grows into times tables. Writing grows from individual letters into sentences. Form drawing moves into running, looping patterns that prepare the hand for cursive.

II.The Main Lesson Blocks

These are the traditional Class 2 main lesson blocks. Schools vary in exactly which they teach and when, but the underlying pattern is consistent worldwide.

Aesop's and Celtic Fables

1–2 blocks · 3–4 weeks each

The fables of Aesop and the older animal tales of the Celtic and Native American traditions. The fox who is too clever, the tortoise who is patient, the lion who learns mercy. Each animal carries a quality of human nature, and the children meet themselves in the menagerie. Discussion is gentle — no "moral of the story" is announced; the pictures do the work.

Legends of the Saints

1–2 blocks · 3–4 weeks each

The lives of saintly figures across cultures — Francis of Assisi who spoke with the wolf, Christopher who carried the child across the river, Brigid of Kildare, Nicholas of Myra, the bodhisattvas of the East. The children meet, in story form, what a fully realised human being looks like. The saints become inner companions for the whole year.

Local Folklore or Native Tales

1 block

Folk tales rooted in the children's own region or in the deeper indigenous traditions of the land. The point is to deepen the child's sense of place — the stories the land itself has told.

Mathematics — Times Tables

3–4 blocks across the year

The multiplication tables from 2 through 12, learned through movement, song, and rhythm. The class skip-counts while jumping, clapping, bouncing balls. Multiplication patterns are discovered visually — the five-times table forms a star, the three-times table a triangle. By the end of Class 2, most children know their times tables not as memorised facts but as patterns they have moved through.

Mathematics — Place Value, Time, Money

1 block

Column addition with carrying, place value, telling time on an analogue clock, simple money calculations. The four operations met in Class 1 now meet real-world quantities.

Form Drawing — Running Forms

weekly throughout the year

Form drawing develops into flowing, repeating patterns — running waves, spirals, plaits. These prepare the hand and eye for the cursive script that will come later in Class 3.

III.A Sample Weekly Rhythm

Within each block, the days of the week each have their own gentle character. Here is a sample rhythm a Class 2 teacher might keep.

A typical week inside a Class 2 block

  • Monday Tell a new fable or saint story; act it out together
  • Tuesday Recall the story; draw a picture; copy a sentence from the board
  • Wednesday Skip-counting and times tables work; introduce a math concept
  • Thursday Painting or running form drawing; story continues or deepens
  • Friday Review the week; share the main lesson books; closing verse

IV.Main Lesson Book — Example Pages

Each block produces its own hand-made book. Here are examples of what Class 2 children might draw and write into their main lesson books across the year.

  • A large drawing of the fox and the grapes, with the fox stretching upward; the child's own retelling of the story underneath.
  • Saint Francis with the wolf at Gubbio, surrounded by townspeople; a verse about peace below.
  • A times-table flower — the multiples of 7 connected by lines around a circle of 12, making a six-pointed star.
  • A running spiral form-drawing pattern, in alternating red and blue, used as a chapter border.

V.The Year's Morning Verse

Each Waldorf main lesson opens with a verse. In Classes 1–4 the children speak the verse for the younger grades, written by Rudolf Steiner; from Class 5 onward they speak the upper-grades verse.

The sun with loving light
makes bright for me each day.
The soul with spirit power
gives strength unto my limbs.
In sunlight shining clear
I revere, O God,
the strength of humankind
that thou so graciously
hast planted in my soul,
that I with all my might
may love to work and learn. — Continuing the Classes 1–4 morning verse

VI.Recommended Books

A small library of resources for teachers, homeschoolers, and curious parents.

  • Aesop's Fables — Milo Winter or Arthur Rackham illustrated editions — Lovely classic editions for telling and retelling
  • Mary Stuart — Tales of Saints and Heroes — A classic Waldorf collection for Class 2
  • Jakob Streit — And There Was Light and others — Saint legends in the Waldorf storytelling tradition
  • Charles Kovacs — The Apostles — Often used in Class 2 or 3
  • Donna Simmons — The Christopherus Class Two Syllabus — Full curriculum for the year
  • Eric Fairman — A Path of Discovery, Vol. 2 — Waldorf curriculum guide for Class 2
