~ what we are making ~

The Waldorf Path Directory

A curated library of substantial guides on Waldorf education, gathered into one place where they can be found, trusted, and read alongside one another.

For more than a hundred years, Waldorf education has grown into a worldwide movement of more than a thousand schools across sixty countries. In that time, an enormous amount has been written — by teachers, by parents, by homeschoolers, by curative educators — about how it works, what it asks of a family, and how to live well within it. But most of that knowledge is scattered: across a hundred school websites, in out-of-print books, on blogs that disappear, in personal teacher notes never shared with anyone outside their school.

The Waldorf Path Directory is an attempt to gather. Substantial, honest, beautifully-presented guides on the questions Waldorf families and teachers actually ask — the festival year, the curriculum grade by grade, the craft traditions, the rites of passage, the harder questions about fit and cost and special needs and screens. Each guide written carefully, in one calm plural voice. Each guide honest about both the gifts of Waldorf education and its limits.

It is not a school. It is not a curriculum. It is not a substitute for the Waldorf teacher's own training, or the homeschool parent's own work, or the conversation with a real community. It is a companion — something you can return to in the quiet moments, when you have a question and would like an honest answer, gathered carefully by people who have done the looking for you.

~ why we are making it ~

A small honest project, made by people who care

The directory began as a personal project — the kind of resource we ourselves wanted when we first encountered Waldorf education and could not find. Too much of what existed online was either thin marketing for individual schools, or scattered enthusiast blogs, or critical voices speaking from outside the tradition.

What was missing was a single place where someone could go to find substantial, honest, carefully-written guides on the questions that come up in actual Waldorf family life — written with warmth, but also with the willingness to name the hard parts and the variations and the genuine limits of the tradition alongside its real gifts.

So we began writing the guides we wished had existed. The Waldorf Path Directory is what those guides have become — and what they are still becoming.
